# Enterprise Escalation Principles

These principles apply to all escalation decisions across Grumble & Crumb operations. They take precedence over any business-unit escalation guidance when there is a conflict.

---

## Core Principle

**Escalate early. Do not wait for a situation to worsen before involving a higher authority.**

The cost of an unnecessary escalation is a brief inconvenience. The cost of a delayed escalation can be significant harm to a customer, associate, or the business.

---

## When to Escalate

Escalate to a Shift Lead or manager when:

1. A situation involves potential physical harm to any person.
2. A situation exceeds your authority (financial, policy, or access).
3. You have attempted a resolution and the customer remains unsatisfied.
4. A situation could attract external attention (media, complaint to ownership).
5. You are unsure whether a situation is safe.
6. Two or more standard resolution options have been tried without success.
7. A policy or procedure does not clearly apply to the situation.

---

## How to Escalate

1. **Notify clearly:** Tell the Shift Lead or manager what has happened and what you have already tried.
2. **Stay present:** Unless directed otherwise, stay with the customer or situation until the Shift Lead arrives.
3. **Hand off cleanly:** Brief the Shift Lead before stepping away. Do not leave the customer without context.
4. **Document:** Log the event in Order Oracle after resolution, regardless of outcome.

---

## What Associates Must Not Do

- Do not attempt to resolve a Level 3 or Level 4 incident without Shift Lead involvement.
- Do not make financial commitments beyond authorized amounts.
- Do not make statements about legal liability, insurance, or regulatory compliance.
- Do not send a customer away without offering a resolution or escalation path.
- Do not resolve an escalation by simply ignoring it and hoping it goes away.

---

## Documentation Requirement

All escalated incidents must be logged in Order Oracle within one hour of resolution. Undocumented escalations cannot be reviewed, learned from, or used to improve processes.
